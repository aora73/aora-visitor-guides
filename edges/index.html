Four down. Three calling.

Australia is not a backdrop. It is a shape. Every shape has a north, a south, an east, a west, a high point, a low point, and a centre you can actually stand on.

Most people drive through the middle of the story. The explorer spirit wants the punctuation. Dingo and AORA have stood at four of the seven. The map still has three blank.

4 of 7 stood

There are seven places on the Australian mainland that settle it. Not islands. Not territories. The continent itself: corner to corner, summit to salt, and the quiet pin where the map balances.

NorthStood here

Cape York

The Tip · Pajinka

The bitumen gave up a long way back. Rainforest, river crossings and one last beach still ask whether you meant it. The Old Telegraph Track, or whatever the wet season has left you, is not a drive you rush. You pick a line, you read the water, and you leave the place the way you found it. Stand at the sign and the whole continent is behind you. Coral Sea in front. That is as far north as this country goes without leaving the ground. The photo is the sign. The point is arriving with the vehicle, and the road home, still intact.

EastStood here

Cape Byron

Byron Bay · New South Wales

The easy one, and that is not an insult. A lighthouse, a headland, the first sunlight on the mainland if you are up for it. You can be there before breakfast if you stay in town, walk the path, and still have the ocean to yourself if you time it. Most easterly rock under your boots, with Byron Bay carrying on just over your shoulder. Surfers, cafes, the whole performance of the coast, and then this quieter cliff where the map actually ends. An extreme does not have to be hard to be real. The map does not grade on difficulty. It only cares that you stood on the last bit of dirt facing the Pacific, and that you noticed.

SouthStill calling

South Point

Wilsons Promontory · Victoria

This one does not care about your bullbar. The southernmost point of the mainland is a walk. A long one, out and back through heath and granite, with the wind off Bass Strait looking for a gap in your jacket. Wilsons Promontory does not offer a carpark at the tip. You leave the vehicle, you carry what you need, and you commit to the return before the weather has finished making up its mind. You earn the tip of Victoria the old way. Feet. Time. Weather that can turn while you are still committed. Tasmania sits further south, which is the argument people start. The mainland does not care. South Point is the argument settled in granite, salt and a sore pair of legs.

WestStill calling

Steep Point

Shark Bay · Western Australia

Cliffs falling into a dark, working ocean, and a track that reminds you why you learned to air down. Steep Point is not a lookout with a sealed road and a kiosk. It is the end of a peninsula, soft sand, sharp rock, and a long way back to Denham if you have misread the day. Westernmost ground on the mainland. Remote enough that turning around is a decision you make with fuel, water and daylight, not with a feeling. The Indian Ocean does not perform for you. It works. This is the point that sorts spectators from travellers. If you only came for the photo, the cliffs will still make you earn the drive out.

HighStood here

Mount Kosciuszko

2,228 metres · Snowy Mountains

Not dramatic by world standards, which is the Australian joke. Our highest ground is a broad, honest summit you can walk to, weather permitting, and still feel the whole south-east laid out under a hard sky. In summer the snow gums and the granite do the talking. In winter the wind does, and the path from Charlotte Pass or Thredbo stops being a stroll the moment the weather turns. Height, here, is earned in wind, not in ego. Stand on the summit and 2,228 metres feels almost modest. The view does not.

LowStill calling

Kati Thanda

Lake Eyre · about 15 m below the sea

Somehow the biggest sky you will ever stand under. More often salt country than water. A white pan, a heat shimmer, and a silence so wide it makes the radio in the car feel rude. When it is water, the desert throws a party the birds hear from three states away. Pelicans where there should be dust. Roads that were fine last season and are not fine this one. The lowest point is not a hole you climb into. It is a horizon that forgot to stop. You do not conquer Kati Thanda. You visit on its terms, and you leave when the country says so.

CentreStood here

Lambert Centre

The balance point · Northern Territory

Not the rock everyone photographs, though that country holds its own kind of heart. Uluru has the crowds and the postcards. This does not. The Lambert Centre is where the geometry of the mainland sits down, marked by a modest frame, a flag and a lot of red dirt. Easy to miss if you are chasing the famous. The track out there looks simple on a map, then asks quiet questions about your tyres, your water and your patience. Impossible to fake once you have driven out there and stood in the actual middle, flies and all. No ticket gate. Just every edge of the country, for a moment, the same distance from your boots.

The good news

South Point is still out there, past the last carpark. Steep Point is still out there, at the end of a soft track and a long cliff. Kati Thanda is still out there, waiting on season, access, and respect for country that does not belong to a checklist.

An explorer spirit is not a personality. It is a practice. Knowing how far to drop your tyre pressures, and when not to. Reading a track, a sky, a fuel gauge and your own pride, then trusting the first three more than the last. Turning up prepared, so the wild place does not have to educate you the expensive way.

You do not have to do all seven. You do not have to do them in order. You do not even need a four-wheel drive to stand at Byron, walk the Prom, or follow the path to Kosciuszko. Some of these points ask for a vehicle and a skillset. Some ask for boots and an early start. All of them ask for the same thing: that the edge of the country is worth your time, and that you go in a way that lets you come home.

If you have the explorer spirit, you already know which one is yours.
